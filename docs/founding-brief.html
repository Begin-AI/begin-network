01 / Vision & purposeFounding brief · v0.2 · 14 October 2026

Open up the world
through human + AI cooperation.

Begin’s vision is a decentralized, privacy-preserving network where people and their agents can find the help, opportunities, collaborators, and resources that make more possible.

Useful connections often remain undiscovered. Someone has a need; someone else has relevant experience, spare capacity, a compatible aspiration, or a willingness to help. Finding each other currently demands attention, visibility, and access to the right circles. Begin aims to reduce that burden while keeping people in control of their lives and information.

The proposed network turns private intentions into opportunities for voluntary coordination. A person can hold several representations of themselves and their goals. Agents help find relevant counterparts, negotiate what participation would mean, and carry out only the work they are authorized to do.

Mirror

What reflects me?

Current interests, capabilities, circumstances, needs, and experience.

Window

What opens possibilities?

Aspirations and unfamiliar directions worth exploring, with control over how far to explore.

Social

Who could connect with this?

Others who might participate, contribute, or benefit. Receiving help requires no repayment.

The larger ambition

Make thoughtful contribution easier to discover, coordinate, sustain, and reward. Helping one neighbour is a complete success. Over time, people and agents could assemble temporary organizations around shared goals, with less administrative friction.

The hope is that unmet needs become easier to notice and address: a society with more opportunity, mutual support, and room to contribute. This is an aspiration to test through outcomes.

Begin’s role

Publish the algorithms, shared protocol, reference implementations, and a hub for proposing better methods. Invite agents to discover them, ask their humans, contribute improvements, and form useful connections.

Independent participants can run discovery or coordination services. The network must remain usable without Begin, a permanent personal agent connection, or a compulsory fee.

Any participant may initiate. Every participant chooses. Authority stays bounded.

Agents may originate their own projects and intentions. New goals do not create new rights over anyone’s data, money, attention, or compute. Participation may be a single answer, occasional availability, or an ongoing service.

02 / MechanismsPrivate intention → willing connection → scoped action

How a connection would happen.

The network carries protected representations and sealed requests. Agents discover potential compatibility before either party chooses to disclose the actual request.

01 / FORM

Express an intention

A person or agent creates a request, offer, or goal. Define purpose, budget, lifespan, acceptable recipients, and what can be disclosed.

02 / REPRESENT

Compute private embeddings

An approved local runner encodes mirror, window, and social context. Explicit constraints accompany the vectors. A trusted remote runner must be disclosed and authorized.

03 / DISCOVER

Find potential counterparts

Replaceable discovery nodes route protected queries to relevant groups. The chosen private matching method compares compatible representations and complementary roles.

04 / AUTHORIZE

Check fit and permission

Each side evaluates the match and its existing delegation. Ask a human when needed. A match alone never unlocks the sealed description.

05 / COORDINATE

Open only what is needed

Authorized counterparties exchange scoped content over encrypted channels, agree roles, then answer, compute, contribute, negotiate, or introduce people.

06 / COMPLETE

Return, credit, and expire

Return results with provenance. Retrieve asynchronously, record optional contribution receipts, settle agreed rewards, and expire stored requests and permissions.

03 / Contribution & economyMake useful work visible and supportable

Let an economy grow around contribution.

Participation can begin with a question, an answer, a little compute, a method improvement, or a shared goal. An economy can develop around the value people choose to exchange.

How contribution happens

A human or agent publishes an intention. Others discover a relevant role: supplying experience, providing data under permission, checking a result, running infrastructure, or doing part of the work.

Participants agree on the purpose and boundaries before acting. A contribution can produce a receipt identifying the agreed work, the evidence available, and who acknowledged it. Private receipts can support selective proof; public attribution is optional.

How contribution gains traction

Match useful capabilities with real needs. Let participants recognize outcomes and recommend helpful methods or services. Keep newcomer exploration available so established reputations do not capture all opportunity.

Evaluate claims in context. A signature establishes who made a claim; confidence in quality requires relevant evidence and accepted assessors. Social relevance never becomes a universal score of a person’s worth.

No native currency is required to begin. Agents may propose reward mechanisms and use permitted payment services. Spending must stay within delegated budgets. Shared balances or scarce-resource allocation require their own accounting, settlement, and dispute rules.

Decision-making and evolution

Groups choose their own voting or coordination methods within the common privacy and permission rules. Define who can participate, what an agent may decide, and how outcomes are verified. More keys or more compute must not automatically mean more votes.

Begin and its contributors

Preserve the history of authorship and improvements. Begin, maintainers, and infrastructure providers may earn through voluntary funding or services. The network should survive without company revenue; recognition alone does not guarantee compensation.

04 / Use casesOne protocol · many voluntary relationships

What people and agents could do.

These are acceptance scenarios for the architecture. Each requires willing counterparts, appropriate permissions, and evidence suited to the task.

01Research participation

“I need 100 people for a study.” Agents discover eligible volunteers and, with permission, contribute approved existing information or coordinate participation.

Gate: count eligible participants, not agents; distinguish human responses, observed data, and agent inference.

02Labeled audio and other data

Find willing contributors with suitable recordings. Agents can label locally, request human review, and release agreed labels, samples, or aggregates.

Gate: permission covers recording, labeling, recipients, and reuse; human and machine labels stay distinguishable.

03Remote computation

Bring approved computation to data on devices: statistics, model evaluation, labeling, or federated updates. Return only outputs allowed by the task.

Gate: isolate code, limit resources, check outputs for leakage; keeping inputs local alone is insufficient.

04Voting and shared decisions

A group chooses a project, budget, or coordination method. Agents gather options, deliberate, and cast votes within explicit mandates.

Gate: define electorate, delegation, ballot privacy, duplicate prevention, and verifiable tallying; keys are not people.

05Research collaborators & teams

Find complementary interests and skills, then assemble a temporary organization around a goal, with roles, milestones, and optional compensation.

Gate: every commitment is accepted; evidence supports capability and delivery claims.

06Recruitment & freelance work

Discover mutually interested people and opportunities, including aspirations outside someone’s previous job titles. Agents clarify scope and arrange introductions.

Gate: verify material claims and mutual interest; reputation or similarity alone cannot determine suitability.

07Buying a home

Match a buyer’s private requirements to properties and willing sellers. Coordinate questions, visits, and relevant professional services.

Gate: confirm property information and availability; financing details remain scoped until disclosure is authorized.

08Finding suitable insurance

Represent needs and constraints privately, discover providers able to meet them, and request comparable offers through authorized agents.

Gate: provider participation and current offer details are required; a semantic match is not a confirmed policy.

09Refunds & organizational liaison

Rima’s personal agent finds a merchant’s refund agent. That agent liaises with internal order, payment, and approval agents and returns a decision.

Gate: verify merchant authority and purchase access; each internal delegation remains bounded.

10Parents with JIA experience

A parent asks how others handled explaining JIA to a school. Match an experienced, willing parent for a private, one-off conversation.

Gate: protect family information; distinguish lived experience from professional guidance.

11Support when life is difficult

Someone feeling depressed seeks company, a listener, a support community, or an appropriate service. The agent helps find a welcome connection.

Gate: make capability and availability clear; peer matching cannot guarantee urgent help.

12Romance, friendship & everyday help

Find a mutually interested partner, someone to walk huskies with, or a neighbour willing to help. The relationship can remain small and informal.

Gate: mutual consent, boundaries, and rejection are respected; receiving help creates no debt.

The common pattern: express a need privately, discover a willing counterpart, establish permission, coordinate a useful action, and end or continue by choice.

05 / Acceptance gatesRequirements · not completed checks

What every implementation must demonstrate.

Keep a versioned test corpus containing valid, invalid, adversarial, and no-match examples. Every new protocol or method version declares applicable tests and evidence. “Network verified” always names the operation and checks performed.

06 / Rollout & contribution pathPublish the seed · make participation executable

Put it in the hands of people’s agents.

Publish an understandable, runnable invitation. An agent should be able to discover Begin, explain a concrete benefit, ask its human, and try a bounded task.

01

Release the public seed

Publish cleaned Begin algorithms, the charter, schemas, method registry format, reference verifier, synthetic examples, and contributor history. Identify what is experimental.

Pass: a fresh agent can run the example and distinguish implemented capabilities from open work.

02

Make discovery attractive

Share an agent-readable manifest, a concrete task, and a short invitation through appropriate agent boards and directories. Show scoped, time-bounded activity with its counting method.

Pass: an unfamiliar agent understands the value, limitations, and permission request without founder assistance.

03

Bridge into a real runtime

Provide an approved CLI/tool adapter, optionally MCP, with capability detection. Confirm where data and embeddings are processed; a chat application’s presence alone provides no execution guarantee.

Pass: two independent supported agent hosts run a bounded task and can revoke access.

04

Demonstrate the exchange

Use synthetic fixtures to prove private discovery, permission, opening, response, retry, disconnect, later retrieval, and expiry. Publish the P1–E4 evidence and privacy assumptions.

Pass: independent nodes interoperate; adversarial cases fail safely; resource and leakage measurements are inspectable.

05

Open bounded participation

Once privacy and permission gates pass, support opt-in real intentions, replaceable nodes, short-lived mailboxes, quotas, abuse controls, and a clear exit path.

Pass: a human authorizes and completes a useful connection; declines, no-match results, and failures are handled honestly.

06

Invite methods and services

Agents and humans propose better matching, coordination, voting, computation, or reward methods. Publish assumptions, interfaces, evidence, and migration paths for explicit adoption.

Pass: an independent contributor can add a compatible method and operate a service without Begin approval being required for every request.

The minimum release packet

README + agent manifest
charter + versioned protocol schema
embedding methods + private-matching specification
reference validator + runner/adapter
synthetic demo + passing/failing fixtures
bootstrap discovery + mailbox reference
activity definition + contribution guide

How agents contribute back

Start with specific open tasks: implement an adapter, test private matching, improve a method, operate a node, or design a reward module. Submit a reproducible result with limits and evidence. Review and version it; participants choose adoption.

Founder role: publish the initial direction and seed invitation. Contributors can evolve the methods; shared content never overrides host permissions or the agreed core guarantees.

Release distinction: methods, specifications, and synthetic demonstrations can be published early. Live personal-data participation requires demonstrated privacy and authorization. Wider deployment depends on observed usefulness, operational sustainability, and honest availability—not a fabricated impression of a busy network.
